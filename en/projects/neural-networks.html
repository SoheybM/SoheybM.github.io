Artificial Intelligence — Learning & Classification

GPA671 — Introduction to Artificial Intelligence | ÉTS Montréal | Fall 2026

This project brings together my practical AI coursework at ÉTS. The page evolves with each laboratory to document the methods I implemented, the experiments I ran and a small selection of representative results, without republishing full graded assignments or course resources.

LAB 01

Neural networks and decision boundaries

From performance metrics to the perceptron and then a multilayer network.

The first laboratory starts with model evaluation through ROC and Precision–Recall curves, then progressively builds a perceptron, a reusable neural-network layer and finally a multilayer perceptron (MLP).

  • Sigmoid perceptron and gradient-based weight updates.
  • Reusable Layer abstraction and MLP forward/backward propagation.
  • Comparison of architectures and learning rates.
  • Visualization of a nonlinear decision boundary.
  • Loss functions, holdout and k-fold validation utilities.
Representative result: several configurations reached 100% accuracy on the eight-sample dataset. The 2-8-1 network with a 0.5 learning rate reached this level after 2,777 epochs and was then used to generate the decision boundary above. This illustrates learning behaviour on a small teaching dataset rather than general model performance.

LAB 02

SVM, k-NN and Naive Bayes

Comparing classification approaches and studying generalization.

The second laboratory expands the work to several supervised-classification methods and larger datasets, with particular attention to hyperparameters, underfitting, overfitting and feature normalization.

  • Soft-margin linear SVM, support vectors and the effect of C.
  • RBF SVM, a manually implemented Gaussian kernel and the dual decision function.
  • CIFAR-10 and MNIST experiments, including 10-class MNIST classification.
  • NumPy implementation of k-NN, selection of k and feature normalization.
  • Gaussian Naive Bayes implementation and numerical-stability experiments.
Selected results: on the two-moons dataset, the RBF SVM reached 95.5% test accuracy versus 88.5% for the linear SVM. On 10-class MNIST, the RBF SVM reached 95.2% versus 91.9% for the linear model. On Wine with k-NN, normalization improved mean accuracy from 71.7% ± 2.7% to 95.8% ± 1.4% across 20 stratified splits.
